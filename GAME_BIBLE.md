# The Boli Bureaucrat — game bible

Version 6.0.0 · 1 November 2026

**Big promises. Tiny islands.** A playful political career tycoon set across the Maldives. Start in a party branch, build a reputation and actual homes, answer rivals, campaign for Parliament, and try for the presidency. The humour comes from oversized egos, chaotic offers and the gap between speeches and delivery.

## Fiction and presentation

Maldivian places inspire the geography. All playable parties, politicians, investors, companies, posts, corruption, misconduct, penalties, finances and election results are invented. Familiar party-style acronyms do not identify the fictional people with real public figures. There is no historical document browser, live political feed or real social posting. Archived development research is excluded from the app and current release packs.

The visual system uses original comic portraits, Fredoka headings, ocean blue framing, cream cards, heavy ink outlines, yellow action buttons and saturated coral, mint and purple accents. Three.js builds stylised island, office, protest, station, meeting, equipment, boat-arrival and overseas scenes. The player has an explicit location and a YOU ARE HERE marker. Buildings change the island scene as construction completes. Night mode, reduced motion and high contrast remain available.

## The weekly loop

1. Hear the current story mission and choose a response.
2. Spend three actions on organising, public service, building, meetings, press, protests, rival replies or personal recovery.
3. Check the inbox for investor gifts and housing deadlines.
4. Advance one week. Local opinion, money, staff, construction, investigations, weather and national politics develop together.
5. Review what changed, collect completed-chapter grants, and prepare the next move.

There are no real-time energy waits, purchasable advantages, advertisements, loot boxes or mandatory network calls. Stamina and stress are strategic resources refreshed through personal time; an empty bank balance has work and support routes.

## Campaign structure

Island Legacy has **512 main decisions in 64 acts**: the original 128 authored decisions and 384 decisions built from 48 distinct eight-beat story arcs. The 128-decision National career and 48-decision First Election career remain selectable. The long route spans grassroots organising, branch competition, council leadership, the parliamentary campaign, legislative work, national organisation, a presidential ticket, election/runoff, and executive or opposition play. Losing an election continues the career. Chapter grants require eight completed decisions and can only be collected once.

The offline encounter director assembles extra situations from current career state, relationships, scrutiny, delivery, disasters and national ambition. Investor gifts and rival posts create further recurring choices. This is local procedural generation, without a hosted language-model dependency.

## Island construction

Each of the 24 destination islands has six community plots. The building pot is a restricted ledger separate from personal wealth and party or campaign money. A fictional neighbourhood cooperative supplies MVR 12,000 and 30 materials once at the start or migration of a pre-construction career.

| Building | Cost MVR | Materials | Weeks | Result |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Boli Homes | 5,000 | 12 | 2 | Four unallocated homes |
| Island clinic | 6,500 | 14 | 3 | Health delivery |
| Neighbourhood jetty | 4,500 | 10 | 2 | Transport delivery |
| Solar canopy | 4,500 | 10 | 2 | Environmental delivery |
| Learning hub | 6,500 | 14 | 3 | Education delivery |
| Fish & ice market | 5,000 | 12 | 2 | Fisheries delivery |

Starting construction uses one action. Completed buildings may be upgraded to level three; an upgrade costs MVR 3,500 × current level, eight materials × current level, one action and two weeks. A housing upgrade adds four further homes. Buying twelve materials costs MVR 1,800 and one action. Personal cash may be contributed to the pot, never freely withdrawn from it. Storm impact pauses local construction.

Housing promises need **four completed, unallocated homes on the promised island**, a visit, and a one-action handover. A policy review cannot fulfil them. Investor housing grants also provide stock. Handing keys over consumes the stock, so the same homes cannot satisfy two promises.

A promise has eight weeks. Once its deadline passes, the player must hand over homes late, ask for four more weeks, or refuse. Extensions cost trust and are limited to two. Refusal costs trust and integrity, leaves a permanent record, and releases the weekly clock. Late handovers receive smaller trust gains. Other service promises can be met through two matching policy reviews or a completed matching building.

## Investor inbox

Fictional CEOs, ministers and suppliers offer earmarked funds, materials or completed housing. Offers arrive during the weekly simulation and after overseas contact meetings. Up to five can be pending; each expires after six weeks. The first regular overseas offer arrives after two elapsed weeks. Later offers arrive every three weeks, and an overseas investor brief can be sent once a week for one action.

| Choice | Community result | Private result |
|---|---|---|
| Use it for the island | Full grant, stock or materials | No private income |
| Split it quietly | Half of the offered resources | Smaller benefit and persistent evidence |
| Keep it for myself | No community resources | Personal benefit and persistent evidence |
| Decline | None | None |

Every choice uses one action, is recorded and cannot be repeated. Money gifts enter the building pot; homes stay attached to their intended island. Taking private benefit is an explicit fictional misappropriation choice, with source-labelled transactions, scrutiny and case consequences.

## Island Buzz

Political enemies publish short fictional posts about promises, wealth, protests, delivery and national ambition. The player can show receipts, use humour, own a problem or clap back. Replying uses one action and persists through saves. Honest responses improve trust, humour attracts attention, and hostile replies add fame but cost trust and raise campaign heat. Rivals can respond the following week. No message is sent to an external platform.

## Movement and scenes

The Maldives strategic map supports island search, support/needs/promises/network layers, travel quotes, boat arrivals and local conversations. Local campaigning and housing handovers require the relevant island. Harbour and office can be entered from the home screen. A planned protest on the current island opens the crowd scene; detention replaces the player scene with the station.

Singapore, Colombo, Dubai and New Delhi host invented development forums. Visits require twenty fame or a party council role, two actions and disclosed campaign fares, including the trip home. Contact meetings use one action and generate an offer. Returning uses one action. Overseas location persists, and local construction/campaigning is unavailable until the player comes home. Real cities do not imply real government or company involvement.

## Politics, polls and government

Local voter cohorts respond separately to organisation, awareness, trust, alignment and delivery. Membership signatures are verified later and are not votes. Polls expose uncertainty and rival support. Parliament has 93 simulated seats and bills move through introduction, committee, coalition and voting stages.

Presidential qualification tracks age, endorsements, running mate and nomination entry. Regional teams, national platform, coalition and local record influence election counts. A majority wins; qualifying leading tickets may face a runoff. Taking office vacates the MP seat. Cabinet nominations face review. Published service budgets must total 100% and govern public delivery.

## Protests, hurdles and disasters

Plans track route tension, steward readiness, crowd and discipline. Negotiating access, training stewards, assembling support and responsible conduct affect outcomes. Poor organisation and escalation can lead to detention. Legal aid and hearings provide a route forward without requiring money or influence. Venue hurdles expire or can be reviewed.

Storm surge, flooding, drought and erosion progress through warning, impact, temporary harbour closure, recovery and review. Readiness and relief alter damage and service outcomes. Travel and construction react to impact. Disasters can be disabled in campaign options and are never live forecasts.

## Money, people and press

Six ledgers cover personal, campaign, party branch, community building, public project and offshore balances. Sources, disclosure, restrictions, lawful overseas savings, household liabilities, evidence, cases and forfeiture matter. Public funds cannot normally be moved into a personal account. Foreign-financed national projects include construction and operating obligations.

Twenty-eight recurring NPCs include advisers, a financial expert, a CEO, a journalist, activists, a lawyer, a fisher, a doctor, ministers, a president and rivals. Relationships and paid expertise have distinct effects. Three-question press conferences share one action; answers can increase trust, make a commitment or inflame criticism. A dated record preserves what the player said.

## Character and saves

Choose a name, age, home, education, skills and an original comic portrait. Optional photos are cropped and resized locally; Android uses the system camera/photo picker. No 3D character customisation is required. Saved careers include choices, finances, projects, promises, relationships and location. v1/v2/v3/v4 saves migrate to v5 without clearing the old career; outdated historical feed entries are removed. JSON backups can be imported or explicitly shared. No game account or cloud AI is needed.

## Living-island expansion

The recurring cast now has 28 fictional adults: twelve new architects, captains, teachers, youth organisers, diaspora and overseas investors, a reef scientist, a construction lead, an arts organiser and a cooperative banker. Each has an original illustrated portrait, personal ambition, individual advice and relationship records. Six new paid specialists add useful retained skills. Existing careers gain contacts without resetting money, old relationships or audio preferences.

Larger naturalistic islands use irregular beaches, vertex-coloured terrain, textured ground, open serviced districts, roads, a village, harbour, mosque, market, planters and lighting. Architecture has foundations, plaster, framed windows, pitched roofs and balconies. Local construction uses the actual saved progress: slabs and reinforcement, rising columns/walls, roof framing and a completed building. Cranes, hooks, excavators and workers move continuously; storms pause the saved work. Buildings can be inspected close up. Palms, ferry traffic, articulated walking people, birds and a lightly animated sea keep the scene alive. Lighting uses standard materials and soft shadows. Static architecture and character parts are batched to reduce draw calls. Reduced motion freezes activity, and covered/background scenes pause. These are illustrative island scenes rather than surveyed 3D replicas.

Nine investor organisations make distinct offers. Four overseas/diaspora funders have Singapore, Sri Lankan and United Arab Emirates settings; they are invented organisations. Offers include larger housing allocations, solar support, cargo materials and cooperative funds. The inbox shows origin, priority, the contact's pitch, and all four choices. Accepting a community offer improves the contact relationship; diversion damages it and leaves evidence. Five offers can wait at once. Outreach uses one action and is limited to once per week.

## Audio

Four original 8-bit-style tracks are synthesised locally: Harbour Hustle (112 BPM), Paperwork Shuffle (98), The Megaphone March (124), and Passport to Possibilities (116). Square/triangle notes, arpeggios, bass and seeded noise drums provide a scene-specific soundtrack. Eleven effect cues cover buttons, confirmations, coins, building, travel, notifications, replies, the weekly clock, success, errors and protests. Separate music/effects controls, volume sliders and a quick master mute are provided. Audio unlocks after a player gesture and pauses when hidden, including native app lifecycle changes. No recordings, third-party melodies, streaming services or network dependency are used. Old mute preferences survive migration.

## First-week onboarding

A new character receives an optional seven-step guide from Aisha. The player joins through the real first mission, listens to residents, starts Boli Homes, and decides a real diaspora investor offer using the usual three actions. The guide then explains key handovers, housing deadlines and rival replies, before advancing the normal weekly clock. It does not add free actions or mark construction complete. Progress saves through reloads. The guide can collapse, skip, or replay from Settings. Replaying cannot generate another tutorial gift.

## Island Legacy campaign director

The AI is an offline, goal-based game director, not a hosted language model. It reads unresolved claims, overdue promises, team wages, service condition and the campaign record to recommend a priority. Three named fictional rivals have ambitions, bounded budgets and momentum; every three weeks they evaluate canvassing, scrutiny, coalition and rally actions. Scores depend on their goals and your record, with seeded variation. Their spent budgets, actions, reasons and memory save with the career. Balanced and relaxed pressure are selectable. There is no API key, network dependency, conversation service or hidden subscription.

Twenty scandal themes arrive through the director. A public claim is labelled unverified. You can publish records, commission a faster independent review, acknowledge a concern, deny it, or privately suppress it. A review checks the available prior-decision evidence; it does not manufacture a legal conviction or erase wrongdoing. Suppression creates persistent evidence. Real related records can be disclosed through review. Ignored claims escalate and increase political heat; resolved unsupported claims can recover trust. Heat affects later national contests. The independent legal/detention system remains active.

## Neighbourhoods, people and office

Each destination begins with six serviced plots. Three district extensions add six each, reaching 24 plots per island and 576 across the 24 destinations. Extensions cost two actions, twenty materials and MVR 12,000 / 24,000 / 36,000 restricted development funds. The six existing building types retain levels one through three. Ready buildings have a condition score, losing 0.35 per week; low-condition services erode local trust/delivery every four weeks. Maintenance costs two materials, one action and MVR 300 per level. A service crew protects condition.

Four finished homes can be allocated for one action through the building desk or a promised-key handover. Households and building occupancy persist. Completed service buildings and occupied housing can introduce paid local organisers. Recruitment costs one action and MVR 300 campaign funds; a building needs four weeks before its next introduction. Twelve given names and family combinations create a cast of up to 48 local recruits in addition to 28 recurring contacts. Each recruit has an origin building, home island, skill, loyalty, assignment and wage record. Weekly wages are MVR 50 each. Canvassers bring small lawful domestic donations and organisation, builders help construction on their home island, service teams support delivery and condition, and rest improves morale. Training improves skills and loyalty. If wages cannot be paid, the recruit becomes inactive and morale falls.

The campaign office is an explorable redesigned 3D interior with glazing, desks, monitors, files, a meeting table, media equipment, reception, refreshments and a ceiling fan. Its six rooms each have three renovation levels. A renovation takes two weeks, one action and campaign funds; there is one active project at a time. Reception/workroom increase crew capacity, archives at level two reduce records-response costs, a studio improves factual press answers, the strategy room adds two weeks per level to accepted side-quest deadlines, and the lounge supports crew morale. Lagoon, timber and midnight finishes are free cosmetic choices. Scaffolding, tarps and a worker appear during an active renovation. Completed rooms change the actual model.

## Recurring side missions and long-term goals

Forty authored brief blueprints can recur across the 24 islands. A brief states a location, actual objective, deadline and separated campaign/development/material reward. Up to six can be active; accepting is free and sets a baseline so earlier work is not counted twice. Reporting back costs one action and requires visiting the island. Objectives include construction, upgrades, delivered keys, local recruitment, maintenance, public listening, policy, press, training and office renovation. New offers can arrive every two weeks, with up to four pending. Offers expire after twenty weeks; accepted work expires after twenty-four weeks plus strategy-room time. Completing or declining creates room for another local priority. Completed grants cannot be claimed twice.

37 milestones reward housing allocations, completed buildings, island visits, side missions, recruits, renovations, review work and service networks. Each grants campaign/development funding and materials once. There is no premium currency, stamina timer, paid skip, advertising reward or real-time waiting.

The 48 later main-story conflicts include housing queues, ferry access, school repairs, fair procurement, relief audits, reef protection, payroll scrutiny, disability access, cooperative elections, inter-island services and a final legacy review. Every arc follows petition, full cost, obstruction, public question, evidence file, inspection, deciding meeting and record. These are explicit authored arc templates with local variations, not 384 claims of completely unrelated handcrafted scenarios.

After the original presidential career, a national contest recurs every 260 game weeks, first at week 820. A continuing ticket needs national support and a manageable public record. A maximum of two presidential terms leads into continuing party leadership and island development. Losing also preserves local work. The 512th decision concludes the main narrative; island development, side missions and the weekly simulation can continue afterward.

## Pacing and the 100-hour goal

The last main decision is scheduled at game week 2677. This gives about 2,678 weekly planning turns and up to 8,034 action slots, with 512 story decisions, recurring briefs, 576 possible construction plots, office/crew development and later mandates. A design estimate of 2.25–3 minutes per planning week yields roughly 100–134 hours for a deliberately thorough run. This is an assumption for balancing, not measured playtime or a minimum duration: a player who skips actions can finish much faster. No artificial wait gate enforces the estimate. A genuine 100-hour retention claim needs human playtests, pacing analysis and further writing as needed. No external analytics is included.

The user’s screenshots exposed a theme cascade bug: light career/chapter panels inherited pale global dark-theme text. Semantic surface, text and muted-text tokens now define each theme together. The profile has a compact rank track and optional milestone ladder; each act has a two-column mission board. Named contrast checks cover the character name, biography, act heading, act description and mission title in both themes. User reference images guide the visual direction; no reference-game artwork is included.

The title is a full-viewport live scene with a Malé-inspired dense skyline, seawall, harbour, village houses and palms. Sky, clouds, distant haze, lagoon depth, wave normals, caustics, foam and sun highlights are shader-driven. The island stays low-lying. Buildings use original procedural facade textures, window details, balconies, roof equipment, planters, coloured plaster, sloped village roofs and taller residential forms. Roads have kerbs and crossings; moving traffic joins pedestrians, boats, cranes and birds. An environment map, standard materials, soft shadows and tone mapping light the scene. Static scenery and character parts are batched. The title actions sit near the bottom over the sea. These are illustrative Maldivian scenes rather than surveyed digital twins, with physical-device frame rates requiring further testing.

Construction now starts with a plot plan: choose a free serviced plot in the scene or the numbered accessible grid, rotate the preview in quarter turns, inspect the quoted cost and confirm. Planning is free; confirmation spends the normal action, community funds and materials. Existing finished buildings can be moved for MVR 600 and one action while preserving occupants, level, assigned recruits and record. Relocation changes spatial clinic coverage and district road demand. Construction advances through foundations, columns/walls and roofing, with height appropriate to the planned tier. Site markers open the actual building’s record. Ten catalogue/blueprint images are rendered from the same Three.js models.

Residential levels now progress from Boli courtyard homes through island townhouses, neighbourhood apartments and harbour residences to a lagoon skyline tower. Capacities are 4, 8, 12, 20 and 32 homes. Levels 2–3 retain the earlier costs. Level 4 needs road level 2, five steel frames, four concrete panels, basic materials and enough power/water/sanitation for the future capacity. Level 5 needs road level 3, eight steel, five concrete and four glass, plus sufficient services. The upgrade adds only the difference in housing capacity; earlier keys and residents remain. Services retain three building levels.

Each island tracks water, power, sanitation, parks, four district roads, supplies, workshop orders, warehouse space and uncollected community revenue. Essential network capacities are 8 initially, then 32 / 128 / 768 homes at levels 1 / 2 / 3. Solar buildings add clean power capacity. Road levels support 24 / 72 / 192 homes per district. Clinic coverage depends on the home and clinic plot coordinates and clinic level. Condition, service coverage, jobs, parks and road pressure affect happiness. Each allocated household represents three simulated residents. Markets, services and commercial specialisation provide local jobs. Green specialisation improves happiness. Specialising completed level-three housing costs MVR 1,800 and one action.

The workshop produces five steel frames in one game week (MVR 450, three basic materials); five concrete panels in two (MVR 550, four materials); or four glass parts in two (MVR 650, two materials and two concrete panels). Ordering costs one action; collecting is free. One work slot is available initially; active local builders expand it to three. Warehouse capacity starts at sixty parts, expands by sixty to 240, and blocks collection when full. Orders use game weeks, never real-time waiting. Public lease/service income accumulates locally to a cap and can be collected only into restricted development funds. It never automatically credits the player’s personal or campaign accounts.

Settings are available from both the title and the in-game menu. Music/effect volumes, master switches, retro/sim music, battery-saver/balanced/high graphics, 30/60 FPS, water animation, shadows, camera motion, an optional FPS counter, reduced motion, night mode and higher contrast are saved. Save now, export, import, guide replay, fullscreen, privacy and credits remain available. Battery saver lowers render resolution, disables shadows and reduces traffic; high detail increases resolution, shadow resolution and traffic. Reduced motion overrides ambient movement and camera easing. No additional paid service or network is needed.

The new icon is an original city-and-ballot island crest in teal, ivory and gold. Web, Play and Android adaptive/legacy launcher assets use the same identity. The existing app package and signing key remain stable for upgrades. Version-5 careers acquire initial networks and graphics preferences while retaining money, homes, recruited people and political choices.

## Building-game research and adaptation

- [EA’s SimCity BuildIt beginner guide](https://help.ea.com/en/articles/simcity/simcity-buildit/beginner-guide/) describes service coverage, residential upgrades, manufactured parts and roads. This project adapts those general loops to separate island development funds and weekly political actions.
- [Gameloft’s City Mania overview](https://www.gameloft.com/blog/players/city-mania-one-minute) describes worker assignments, specialisation and adjacent-building fusion. This project adapts worker-supported production and housing specialisation; it does not implement fusion or reproduce Gameloft assets.
- [Village City Island Sim’s official description](https://www.villagecityislandsim.com/) connects jobs, decoration and resident happiness. This project uses local jobs, public space and services as inputs to simulated happiness.

These are design references, not partnerships. The new mechanics, costs, art and future political outcomes are fictional. Existing 512-decision campaign and 100-hour pacing caveats continue to apply.
